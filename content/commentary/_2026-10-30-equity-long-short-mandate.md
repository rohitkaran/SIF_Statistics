*Second in a series on how each SIF strategy behaved in September's 6% fall, judged against its
mandate rather than the index. ([Part one: hybrids](/commentary/2026-10-29-hybrid-long-short-mandate).)*

An equity long-short fund is the most directional of the SIF strategies: net-long equity, but with
a short book that is meant to cushion the falls. The promise is asymmetry — **participate on the way
up, fall materially less on the way down.** So the test is not just "did it fall less than the
NIFTY" (it mostly will, by construction) but "is the up capture meaningfully larger than the down
capture?" When the two are equal, you are paying long-short fees for index exposure.

September's −6.06% pulled the category apart. The median equity long-short fell **3.9%** — a real
cushion — but the spread underneath that number is the story.

| Fund (Direct–Growth) | Sep return | Down capture | Up capture | Up − Down | Max drawdown |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Arthaya Equity L/S | −2.88% | 35% | 29% | −6 | 5.3% |
| qsif Equity L/S | −1.56% | 57% | 75% | +18 | 12.4% |
| DynaSIF Equity L/S | −4.67% | 54% | 62% | +8 | 8.9% |
| iSIF Equity L/S | −3.02% | 62% | 74% | +12 | 7.7% |
| Titanium Equity L/S | −3.68% | 67% | 76% | +9 | 6.4% |
| Summit Equity L/S | −3.96% | 69% | 88% | +19 | 7.3% |
| Diviniti Equity L/S | −6.29% | 54% | 43% | −11 | 14.6% |
| Sapphire Equity L/S | −5.43% | 86% | 85% | −1 | 8.8% |
| WSIF Equity L/S | −4.64% | 86% | 94% | +8 | 9.7% |

*(Altiva and Apex Equity L/S are omitted from the September column — both lack a full prior month of
NAVs; Altiva's lifetime capture of 81% up / 96% down is itself the wrong asymmetry.)*

**The genuinely hedged.** Arthaya stands out for defence — it captured more of the downside offset
than the upside, the mark of a book run cautiously; it gave up only 2.9% in a 6% month. Summit and
qsif show the other good shape: high up capture with a visibly lower down capture (a +18–19 gap),
i.e. they keep most of the rally and surrender less of the fall.

**The ones paying fees for beta.** Sapphire captured 86% of the downside and 85% of the upside —
within rounding, that is the market. WSIF is close behind. For a strategy whose entire premise is
the short book, an up-minus-down spread near zero is the real failure, independent of the −5% print.

**The worst asymmetry of all.** Diviniti fell **6.3%**, in line with the NIFTY, and sits on the
group's deepest drawdown (≈15%) — and over its life it has captured *more* of the market's downside
(54%) than its upside (43%). That is the opposite of what the structure is for.

The takeaway for equity long-short: in a flat month every one of these looks fine. September showed
which managers are actually running a hedge and which are running the index with a short label on
it. Read the up-minus-down column before the headline return.
