*Third in a series on how each SIF strategy behaved in September's 6% fall, judged against its
mandate. ([Part one: hybrids](/commentary/2026-10-29-hybrid-long-short-mandate) ·
[Part two: equity long-short](/commentary/2026-10-30-equity-long-short-mandate).)*

The active asset allocator is the most flexible mandate in the SIF universe: shift net market
exposure dynamically — lean in when the odds are good, hedge down when they aren't. Done well, it
produces the cleanest shape of all — high participation in rallies, little participation in falls.
It is a small group (three schemes with a full month of NAVs), but September gave it the widest
spread of outcomes relative to its own promise.

| Fund (Direct–Growth) | Sep return | Down capture | Up capture | Volatility | Max drawdown |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| qsif Active Asset Allocator | **+13.75%** | 6% | 77% | 12.8% | 6.7% |
| iSIF Active Asset Allocator | −1.46% | 29% | 37% | 4.4% | 3.2% |
| DynaSIF Active Asset Allocator | −0.78% | 22% | 28% | 4.8% | 2.6% |

**The textbook execution.** qsif's allocator is what the category is supposed to look like on a bad
day: it kept pace with 77% of the market's up moves over its life yet surrendered only **6%** of the
down moves — and in September that translated into a **+13.75%** gain while the index fell 6%. The
trade-off is volatility (12.8%): this is an active, high-conviction book, not a low-beta parking
spot. But the asymmetry is the best in the universe, and it showed up exactly when it was supposed
to.

**The steady hands.** iSIF and DynaSIF ran the conservative version — downside capture in the
low-to-high 20s%, volatility under 5%, drawdowns under 3.5%. Both gave up around a percent in
September. They won't produce a +14% month, but they also won't surprise you; their shape is
consistent with a fund that keeps net exposure modest and moves it gradually.

For allocators the September read is encouraging: all three protected, and the one that leaned in
was positioned correctly. The thing to watch over time is consistency — a 6% down capture is superb,
but the test of an allocator is whether it can *also* be there for the next rally without having
de-risked too early. One month, however good, does not settle that.
