September gave SIF managers the one thing a hedged product is actually built for: a falling
market. The NIFTY 50 lost **6.06%** on the month and the NIFTY Midcap 50 **7.91%**, with
realised volatility jumping to roughly 10% and 14% annualised respectively. After a soft August,
this was a clean risk-off stretch — exactly the environment in which a long-short fund is supposed
to prove its worth.

## Why "beat the NIFTY" is the wrong scorecard

It is tempting to note that **29 of the 30** SIF schemes with a full month of NAVs finished ahead
of the NIFTY in September. It is also close to meaningless. A fund that runs, say, 30% net long
*should* fall far less than a fully-invested index when the market drops — clearing that bar is the
starting point, not the result. The honest question is narrower: **did each fund behave the way its
own category promises?**

| Category | What it is built to do | The metric that matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hybrid Long-Short | Preserve capital — "can't lose big" | Very low downside capture, low volatility, shallow drawdown |
| Active Asset Allocator | Shift net exposure dynamically; protect in falls | Low downside capture with asymmetric up/down |
| Ex-Top 100 SMID L/S | Long-short the small- and mid-cap space | Cushion the *Midcap* index, not the NIFTY |
| Equity Long-Short | Net-long but genuinely hedged equity | Participate on the way up, fall materially less on the way down |

"Downside capture" below is the share of the NIFTY's down-day moves a fund absorbed, measured over
each scheme's full NAV history; the September figure is the calendar-month return.

## Hybrids: most did the job — but check the label

As a group the hybrids behaved exactly as advertised: the median hybrid captured just **11%** of the
NIFTY's down days and 25% of its up days, at **3.4% volatility**. In September most finished within a
whisker of flat — Prism **+0.53%**, RedHex **+0.51%**, Arudha **+0.35%**, Apex **−0.10%**, Altiva
**−0.72%**. RedHex and Arudha even carry *slightly negative* downside capture: they tend to tick up
when the market falls. That is the mandate, delivered.

Two names did not behave like capital-preservation vehicles at all. **iSIF Hybrid** has absorbed
**66%** of the NIFTY's downside over its life (12% volatility, a 9% drawdown), and **Titanium
Hybrid** 48% — equity-like risk wearing a hybrid badge. Both fell around 3% in September. If you
bought a "hybrid long-short" expecting it to hold the line, these two didn't.

**qsif Hybrid** sits at the opposite pole, up **+16.9%** on the month. It earned it — but it is no
quiet preserver either: 10.5% volatility and a 6% drawdown put it in a more aggressive bucket than
the category median. It got the calls right; just size it knowing it can move.

## Allocators: the month's standout, plus two steady hands

**qsif's Active Asset Allocator** was the headline alongside its hybrid sibling: **+13.75%**,
capturing 77% of the market's up moves but only **6%** of its down moves — the cleanest asymmetry in
the universe, albeit at 13% volatility. The other two allocators ran the quieter version of the same
playbook: DynaSIF **−0.78%** and iSIF **−1.46%**, both with downside capture in the low-20s%.

## Equity long-shorts: a directional job, very unevenly hedged

This is where the mandate is loosest, and September pulled the group apart. The median equity
long-short fell **3.9%** — less than the index, as it should. But the *quality* of the hedge ranges
enormously. **Arthaya** (29% up / 35% down capture) and **qsif Equity L/S** (75 / 57) show real,
useful asymmetry. Several others barely hedge at all: **Sapphire** has captured 85% of the upside
*and* 86% of the downside — little different from simply holding the market; **WSIF** 94 / 86; and
**Altiva Equity L/S** captures *more* on the way down (96%) than on the way up (81%) — the wrong
asymmetry entirely. **Diviniti** fell in line with the NIFTY (**−6.29%**) and sits on the deepest
drawdown in the group (~15%). For products that charge for the ability to short, the disappointment
of the month was not the loss — it was the absence of protection.

## Ex-Top 100 SMID: measured against the right yardstick, a genuine cushion

Judged against the Midcap 50's −7.9% — the correct benchmark for a small/mid book, not the
large-cap NIFTY — this group's **−2.7%** median is a real cushion. **DynaSIF Ex-Top 100** even eked
out **+0.40%** (96% up capture, 54% down). The laggards, WSIF **−4.35%** and Altiva **−4.69%**, carry
~78% downside capture — closer to owning the midcaps outright than to hedging them.

## The takeaway

September rewarded the funds that were actually hedged and exposed the ones that weren't — whatever
the "long-short" on the tin said. The hybrids and allocators, as a class, earned their mandate;
a cluster of nominally-hedged schemes revealed they are carrying far more market risk than their
category implies. In a quiet month that gap is invisible. In a 6% month it is the whole story.

*Metrics computed from AMFI SIF NAVs (Direct–Growth plans) and NIFTY 50 / Midcap 50 closes.
Up/down capture is measured over each fund's full NAV history and is noisier for very recently
launched schemes. Past performance is not indicative of future results; this note is research
commentary, not investment advice.*
