The Ex-Top 100 long-shorts fish outside the large-cap pool — in small- and mid-caps — so comparing
them to the NIFTY 50 flatters them. The right benchmark is the **NIFTY Midcap 50, which fell 7.91%
in September** (and is a far wilder ride than the large-caps). The mandate: take long-short positions
across the SMID space and cushion that volatility. The capture ratios below are therefore measured
**against the Midcap 50**, not the NIFTY.

On that fair yardstick the group did its job: the median Ex-Top 100 fund fell just **2.7%** against
the midcaps' −7.9%. But these are structurally more volatile than the hybrids (13%+ volatility is
normal here), and the protection was uneven.

| Fund (Direct–Growth) | Sep return | Down capture | Up capture | Max drawdown |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DynaSIF Ex-Top 100 | **+0.40%** | 58% | 105% | 8.3% |
| qsif Ex-Top 100 | −1.18% | 55% | 68% | 14.2% |
| iSIF Ex-Top 100 | −2.68% | 62% | 64% | 10.4% |
| WSIF Ex-Top 100 | −4.35% | 68% | 79% | 10.6% |
| Altiva Ex-Top 100 | −4.69% | 86% | 102% | 12.0% |

*(Capture vs the NIFTY Midcap 50. Apex and Magnum Ex-Top 100 are omitted from September — both lack
a full prior month of NAVs.)*

**The best of the group.** DynaSIF Ex-Top 100 actually finished the month *up* 0.4% while the
midcaps fell 8%. Its shape explains why: it has captured essentially all of the midcap upside (105%)
but only 58% of the downside — the asymmetry you want from a SMID long-short, if you can tolerate
the 15%+ volatility that comes with it. qsif (55% down / 68% up) runs a similar, slightly gentler
version.

**The ones that mostly own the midcaps.** Altiva Ex-Top 100 captured 86% of the midcap downside and
*102%* of the upside — that is close to a leveraged long midcap position, not a hedge; it fell 4.7%.
WSIF (68% down) sits between the two camps. iSIF is roughly symmetric (62/64) — it cushions the
level of the fall but offers little selective protection.

The honest read for this category: against the *correct* benchmark, every one of these funds softened
a brutal month for the midcaps, so none "failed" outright. But the gap between DynaSIF's 58% downside
capture and Altiva's 86% is the difference between a hedged SMID book and an expensive long one. If
you are paying long-short fees to be in small- and mid-caps, that spread is what you are paying for.

**Across the whole series**, one pattern held in every category: in September the returns were almost
a sideshow. What separated the funds was whether their *shape* — low downside capture, real
asymmetry — matched the promise on the label. The best SIFs in each group had it; a handful, in every
category, did not.
