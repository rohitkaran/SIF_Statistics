# The five SIF strategy families, explained simply

Every SIF in India belongs to one of five SEBI-defined strategy families. They share one core idea —
they can **buy stocks they like *and* bet against stocks they don't** — but they differ in *what* they
trade and *how much* risk they take. Here's each in plain English.

## First, what does "long-short" mean?
- **Long** = you *own* a stock; you make money if it rises.
- **Short** = you *bet against* a stock (usually via derivatives); you make money if it falls.

Running both at once lets a fund profit in rising *and* falling markets, and hedge — so it can fall
less than the market when things turn down. That downside cushion is the point.

## 1. Equity Long-Short
**What it does:** mostly owns stocks it expects to outperform, and shorts stocks it expects to lag.
**Where returns come from:** the *spread* between the longs and the shorts — i.e., the manager's
stock-picking skill on **both** sides — rather than the overall market direction.
**Character:** equity-like returns, but usually with lower ups-and-downs than a pure equity fund.

## 2. Hybrid Long-Short
**What it does:** blends equity, debt and derivatives. Often keeps a big chunk in **bonds** and
**market-neutral arbitrage** for steady income, with a smaller hedged-equity sleeve for growth.
**Where returns come from:** interest income + low-risk arbitrage + a modest equity kicker.
**Character:** the *steadiest* family — lower risk, lower expected return, smoother ride. A common
entry point for cautious SIF investors.

## 3. Equity Ex-Top-100 Long-Short
**What it does:** the same long-short idea, but focused on **mid- and small-caps outside the largest
100 stocks**. It buys promising smaller companies and shorts the weak ones.
**Where returns come from:** inefficiency in less-researched smaller companies — more opportunity, but
more volatility.
**Character:** the most aggressive/return-seeking of the equity families.

## 4. Active Asset Allocator Long-Short
**What it does:** dynamically shifts money across **equity, debt and commodities** depending on the
outlook, using long-short and hedging to manage downside.
**Where returns come from:** getting the **asset-class mix** right (rotation) *plus* instrument
selection within each.
**Character:** a "go-anywhere" multi-asset fund; returns depend heavily on the manager's macro calls.

## 5. Sector Rotation Long-Short
**What it does:** rotates between **sectors** — going long the sectors it expects to lead and short
those it expects to lag.
**Where returns come from:** correctly timing which sectors are in and out of favour.
**Character:** thematic and concentrated; fewer funds in this family so far.

## How to compare across families
Don't compare a steady **hybrid** fund to an aggressive **ex-top-100** fund on raw return alone — they
take very different risk. Instead look at:
- **Sharpe / Sortino** — return per unit of risk.
- **Up / down capture vs the Nifty** — how it behaves in rising vs falling markets.
- **Max drawdown & recovery** — worst fall and time to bounce back.
- **The holdings & net exposure** — what it's actually long and short right now.
