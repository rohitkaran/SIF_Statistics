# What is a Specialized Investment Fund (SIF)?

If you've started hearing about **SIFs** and wondered where they fit between a mutual fund and a
PMS, this is the plain-English guide. No jargon — and where we do use a term, we define it.

## The one-line answer
A **Specialized Investment Fund (SIF)** is a new category of investment product introduced by SEBI
(the market regulator) in 2025. It sits **between mutual funds and PMS/AIF**: run by the same mutual
fund houses you already know, but allowed to use **more advanced strategies** — including
**short-selling** — with a **higher minimum investment of ₹10 lakh**.

Think of it as: *mutual-fund-grade governance, with hedge-fund-style flexibility, for informed
investors.*

## Why did SEBI create SIFs?
There was a big gap in the market. On one side, **mutual funds** are simple, cheap and accessible
(₹500 SIPs) but can mostly only *buy* — they can't meaningfully bet against a stock or hedge a
falling market. On the other side, **PMS (₹50 lakh min)** and **AIF (₹1 crore min)** offer advanced
strategies but are out of reach for most people.

SIFs fill the middle: sophisticated strategies at a **₹10 lakh** entry point, inside the trusted,
well-regulated mutual-fund framework (same trustees, custodians and disclosure norms).

## What makes a SIF different from a mutual fund?
1. **They can go "short".** A SIF can profit when a stock *falls*, not just when it rises — usually by
   using derivatives. This lets it hedge and aim for steadier returns when markets drop.
2. **They use "long-short" and allocator strategies.** More on the five families below.
3. **Higher minimum (₹10 lakh)** and aimed at **informed/affluent investors** who understand the
   extra risk.
4. **Higher risk and complexity.** More tools means more ways to be right *and* wrong.
5. **Separate branding.** Each fund house runs its SIF under a distinct name (e.g. Apex by Aditya
   Birla, Titanium by Tata, iSIF by ICICI Prudential, Magnum by SBI, qsif by Quant).

## The five SIF strategy families
SEBI allows these strategy types (we explain each in a separate post):
- **Equity Long-Short** — buy stocks expected to rise, short those expected to fall.
- **Hybrid Long-Short** — a mix of equity, debt and derivatives for steadier, hedged returns.
- **Equity Ex-Top-100 Long-Short** — the same idea, focused on mid/small-caps outside the largest 100.
- **Active Asset Allocator Long-Short** — dynamically shifts across equity, debt and commodities.
- **Sector Rotation Long-Short** — rotates between sectors, long the strong, short the weak.

## Who is a SIF for?
- Investors who **already have a core mutual-fund portfolio** and want a differentiated, hedged sleeve.
- People comfortable with **higher risk, higher complexity, and a ₹10 lakh commitment**.
- Those who value **downside cushioning** — many SIFs aim to fall *less* than the market (you can
  measure this with "down capture", which we track on SIFintel).

It is **not** a starter product, an emergency fund, or a place for money you'll need soon.

## How do you evaluate a SIF?
Because SIFs are new and use complex strategies, look beyond headline returns:
- **Risk-adjusted returns** (Sharpe, Sortino) — return *per unit of risk*, not just return.
- **Drawdown & recovery** — how far it fell and how fast it bounced back.
- **Up/down capture vs the Nifty** — did it rise with the market and, crucially, fall *less* when the
  market dropped?
- **The actual holdings & strategy** — what's it long, what's it short, how is it really making money?
