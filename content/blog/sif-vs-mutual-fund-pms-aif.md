# SIF vs Mutual Fund vs PMS vs AIF

India now has four main ways to invest through a professionally managed pool. They differ mostly on
two things: **how much you need to start**, and **how much the manager is allowed to do**. Here's the
map.

## The quick comparison

| | **Mutual Fund** | **SIF** | **PMS** | **AIF** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Minimum | ₹500 (SIP) | **₹10 lakh** | ₹50 lakh | ₹1 crore |
| Can short-sell? | No (limited) | **Yes** (via derivatives) | Limited | Yes |
| Strategies | Long-only | **Long-short, allocator, sector rotation** | Discretionary equity/debt | Very wide (hedge, PE, VC, debt) |
| You own | Fund units | Fund units | Individual stocks (your demat) | Fund units |
| Regulation/disclosure | Highest, standardized | **High (mutual-fund-grade)** | Moderate | Lighter, private |
| Best for | Everyone | **Informed investors wanting hedged strategies** | HNIs wanting a bespoke stock portfolio | UHNIs / institutions |

## The intuition
- **Mutual fund** = simple, cheap, long-only. The default for almost everyone.
- **SIF** = the mutual fund's "advanced mode." Same governance, but it can hedge and go short, for a
  ₹10 lakh minimum.
- **PMS** = a manager runs a portfolio of stocks *in your own demat account*; more personal, higher entry.
- **AIF** = the widest mandate (true hedge funds, private equity, venture, structured debt), for the
  largest investors.

## Where a SIF genuinely differs
The headline feature is the **ability to short and hedge**. In a falling market, a long-only mutual
fund largely goes down with it. A well-run SIF can **cushion the fall** — or even rise — because its
short positions gain when those stocks drop. On SIFintel you can see this directly as **"down
capture"**: how much of the market's fall a fund actually took. Many SIFs post *negative* down-capture
in stress weeks — meaning they rose while the index fell.

The trade-off: **more tools = more complexity and more risk.** Shorting, leverage and derivatives can
work against a fund just as they work for it. That's the whole reason the minimum is ₹10 lakh and the
product is aimed at informed investors.

## Which should you pick?
- Building your core, or new to investing? **Mutual funds.**
- Have a solid core already and want a **hedged, differentiated sleeve** at a reasonable entry point?
  **A SIF** may fit — chosen carefully.
- Want a **bespoke, direct-ownership** stock portfolio and can commit ₹50 lakh+? **PMS.**
- ₹1 crore+ and want the widest strategy universe? **AIF.**

It isn't either/or. Most SIF investors use it *alongside* mutual funds, not instead of them.

## How to choose a SIF once you're there
Compare on **risk-adjusted return** (Sharpe/Sortino), **drawdown**, **up/down capture vs the Nifty**,
and the **actual strategy and holdings** — not just the headline return. That's what **SIFintel**
puts side by side for every SIF in the market.
