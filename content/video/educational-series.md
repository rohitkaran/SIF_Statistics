## Module 1 — The basics

### E1. What is a SIF? (cornerstone)
**Hook:** "There's a new way to invest in India that your mutual fund simply can't do."
**VO:** A Specialized Investment Fund — a SIF — is a new SEBI category launched in 2025. Think of it as
your mutual fund's *advanced mode*: run by the same fund houses, under the same tight rules, but allowed
to do one big extra thing — *bet against* stocks, not just buy them. That lets it hedge and aim for a
steadier ride. The trade-off: it's for informed investors — a ₹10 lakh minimum and higher risk. It sits
between mutual funds and PMS. That's the SIF.
**Captions:** "Meet the SIF" · "Your fund's advanced mode" · "It can go SHORT ↓" · "₹10 lakh min · higher risk" · "Between MF & PMS"
**Higgsfield:** particles form "SIF"; a mutual-fund UI upgrades to a darker pro terminal; a down-arrow lights up as a position profits.
**Tags:** #SIF #SpecializedInvestmentFund #InvestingIndia #SIFintel

### E2. Why did SEBI create SIFs?
**Hook:** "There was a huge gap in Indian investing — SIFs fill it."
**Points:** Mutual funds = simple, cheap, long-only (can't really hedge). PMS/AIF = advanced but ₹50L–₹1cr
minimums, out of reach. SIFs put sophisticated strategies at a **₹10 lakh** entry, inside the trusted
mutual-fund framework (same trustees, custodians, disclosures).
**Higgsfield:** a 3-tier ladder — Mutual Fund → **SIF** (highlighted) → PMS/AIF; a gap fills with light.
**Tags:** #SIF #SEBI #InvestingIndia #SIFintel

### E3. The 5 SIF categories (overview)
**Hook:** "Every SIF is one of just five types. Here they are in 40 seconds."
**Points:** 1) **Equity Long-Short** — buy winners, short losers. 2) **Hybrid Long-Short** — equity + debt +
hedging, the steadiest. 3) **Equity Ex-Top-100 Long-Short** — the same, in mid/small-caps. 4) **Active Asset
Allocator** — shifts across equity/debt/commodities. 5) **Sector Rotation** — long strong sectors, short weak.
**Higgsfield:** five clean icon cards flipping in sequence, each with its name.
**Tags:** #SIF #LongShort #InvestingBasics #SIFintel

## Module 2 — The five categories (one video each)

### E4. Equity Long-Short, explained
Buys stocks it expects to rise, shorts stocks it expects to fall. Returns come from the **spread** between
the two — stock-picking skill — not overall market direction. Equity-like returns, usually smoother.
**Higgsfield:** two arrows (green up, red down) crossing; the gap between them fills with light.

### E5. Hybrid Long-Short, explained
Blends equity, debt and derivatives — often lots of bonds + market-neutral arbitrage for steady income,
plus a small hedged-equity sleeve. The **steadiest** family: lower risk, lower expected return, smoother ride.
**Higgsfield:** a balanced beam/scale; calm water vs choppy water.

### E6. Equity Ex-Top-100 Long-Short, explained
The long-short idea applied to **mid/small-caps outside the largest 100 stocks**. More opportunity in
less-researched names, more volatility. The most return-seeking of the equity families.
**Higgsfield:** a magnifying glass moving past the "top 100" to smaller glowing companies.

### E7. Active Asset Allocator Long-Short, explained
A "go-anywhere" multi-asset fund that **shifts across equity, debt and commodities** with the outlook,
using hedging to manage downside. Returns depend on getting the asset mix right.
**Higgsfield:** three buckets (equity/debt/commodity) with flows rebalancing between them.

### E8. Sector Rotation Long-Short, explained
Rotates between **sectors** — long the ones it expects to lead, short the laggards. Thematic and
concentrated; timing sector cycles is the whole game.
**Higgsfield:** a wheel of sector icons turning; strong sectors glow, weak ones dim.

## Module 3 — The concepts (jargon-busters)

### E9. "Long" vs "Short" in 30 seconds
Long = you *own* it, win if it rises. Short = you *bet against* it (via derivatives), win if it falls.
Doing both lets a fund make money in rising *and* falling markets.
**Higgsfield:** green up-arrow + red down-arrow, simple 3D icons.

### E10. What is hedging? (why a SIF can fall *less*)
Hedging = holding offsetting positions so a market drop hurts less. A short position gains when stocks
fall, cushioning the portfolio. That's why a hedged fund can fall less than the index — the core SIF idea.
**Higgsfield:** an umbrella over a portfolio as red rain (a market drop) falls.

### E11. Up-capture & down-capture (the concept)
On days the market rises, *up-capture* = what fraction of that rise a fund captured. On days it falls,
*down-capture* = how much of the fall it took. **Below 100% down-capture = fell less than the market.**
(Explain the idea generically — don't name funds.)
**Higgsfield:** a gauge swinging between "up" and "down" zones as a market line moves.

### E12. Sharpe & Sortino, simply
Sharpe = reward for the risk taken — extra return above a safe deposit, per unit of ups-and-downs.
Sortino is the same but only counts *downside* wobble. Higher = better. Return alone isn't enough.
**Higgsfield:** a speedometer/dial; two funds with same return but different "wobble".

### E13. Drawdown & recovery
Drawdown = the worst peak-to-trough fall. Recovery = how long to climb back. Smaller drawdown + faster
recovery = a calmer ride. It's the number that tells you how bumpy the journey was.
**Higgsfield:** an "underwater" curve dipping and climbing back to the surface.

## Module 4 — Context & caution

### E14. SIF vs Mutual Fund vs PMS vs AIF
Where each fits by **minimum** and **freedom**: MF (₹500, long-only) → **SIF (₹10L, can hedge/short)** →
PMS (₹50L, direct stocks) → AIF (₹1cr, widest mandate). Most people use a SIF *alongside* mutual funds.
**Higgsfield:** a clean comparison table animating in row by row.

### E15. How SIFs disclose their portfolios — and how to read one
SIFs disclose holdings **bi-monthly** (a SEBI rule). A disclosure shows each holding, its weight, sector,
and the long/short book. Walk through *how to read* a generic portfolio statement (no specific fund).
**Higgsfield:** a portfolio "X-ray" — holdings bars, an allocation donut, a long/short split (unlabeled/sample).

### E16. Who a SIF is for — and the risks
For informed investors with a core portfolio already, comfortable with ₹10 lakh and higher complexity.
**Not** a starter product or emergency money. Shorting, leverage and derivatives cut both ways — spell out
the risks honestly.
**Higgsfield:** a checklist ticking "for / not for"; a balanced risk-reward scale.

Rapid-fire definitions: NAV · long/short · hedge · arbitrage · net exposure · drawdown · Sharpe · capture.
