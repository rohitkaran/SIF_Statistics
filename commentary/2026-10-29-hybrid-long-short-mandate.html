Hybrid long-short SIFs in September — the category that is supposed to be boring

A hybrid long-short fund has one job — don't lose big. Measured that way, September was a quiet success for most: the median hybrid absorbed just 11% of the NIFTY's fall at 3.4% volatility. But two "hybrids" behaved like equity funds, and one behaved like a hedge fund. Part one of a per-category series.

First in a short series examining how each SIF strategy behaved in September's 6% market fall — judged against what the category is built to do, not against the index.

The hybrid long-short category exists to do one unglamorous thing: preserve capital. It blends equity, debt and hedges so that, in a falling market, it falls very little — and in a rising one, it participates modestly. The right scorecard is therefore not return but downside capture (how much of the market's fall it absorbs), volatility, and drawdown. A good hybrid should post a single-digit downside capture, low-single-digit volatility, and a drawdown you could sleep through.

September — NIFTY 50 −6.06% — was the test. As a group, the hybrids passed comfortably: the median scheme captured just 11% of the index's down days and 25% of its up days, at 3.4% annualised volatility. Most finished the month within a whisker of flat.

The textbook cases. Prism, RedHex and Arudha are the mandate drawn to spec — downside capture at or below 1%, volatility under 2.5%, drawdowns measured in basis points. RedHex and Arudha actually carry negative downside capture: on average they tick up when the NIFTY falls. Apex and Infinity sit just behind, around 8–11% downside capture. None of these will shoot the lights out in a bull run, and that is precisely the deal.

The outlier that earned it. qsif Hybrid gained 16.9% in the month — real, built on a fortnight-long climb, not a data blip. But with 10.5% volatility and a 6% drawdown it is not a quiet preserver; it is an aggressive book that happened to have the right positioning. Own it knowing it can move both ways.

The two that aren't really hybrids. Titanium (48% downside capture) and iSIF Hybrid (66%, with 12% volatility and a 9% drawdown) behave like equity funds wearing a hybrid label. Both fell around 3% — not a disaster, but if you bought either expecting the "can't lose big" profile of the names above, September was a reminder to read the capture numbers, not the category.

The lesson of the month for this group is simple: the label "hybrid long-short" spans a fund that barely moves and a fund that moves like the market. The downside-capture column tells you which one you actually own.
