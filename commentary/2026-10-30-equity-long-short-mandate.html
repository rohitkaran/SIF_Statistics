Equity long-short SIFs in September — where "hedged" meant very different things

An equity long-short fund should rise with the market and fall a lot less. In September the category cushioned the drop as a group — but the hedge quality ranged from genuinely asymmetric to funds that simply owned the market. Part two of a per-category series.

SIFintel Research · 30 October 2026
monthly-reportequity-long-shortdownside-captureseptemberindia

Second in a series on how each SIF strategy behaved in September's 6% fall, judged against its mandate rather than the index. ([Part one: hybrids](/commentary/2026-10-29-hybrid-long-short-mandate).)

An equity long-short fund is the most directional of the SIF strategies: net-long equity, but with a short book that is meant to cushion the falls. The promise is asymmetry — participate on the way up, fall materially less on the way down. So the test is not just "did it fall less than the NIFTY" (it mostly will, by construction) but "is the up capture meaningfully larger than the down capture?" When the two are equal, you are paying long-short fees for index exposure.

September's −6.06% pulled the category apart. The median equity long-short fell 3.9% — a real cushion — but the spread underneath that number is the story.

(Altiva and Apex Equity L/S are omitted from the September column — both lack a full prior month of NAVs; Altiva's lifetime capture of 81% up / 96% down is itself the wrong asymmetry.)

The genuinely hedged. Arthaya stands out for defence — it captured more of the downside offset than the upside, the mark of a book run cautiously; it gave up only 2.9% in a 6% month. Summit and qsif show the other good shape: high up capture with a visibly lower down capture (a +18–19 gap), i.e. they keep most of the rally and surrender less of the fall.

The ones paying fees for beta. Sapphire captured 86% of the downside and 85% of the upside — within rounding, that is the market. WSIF is close behind. For a strategy whose entire premise is the short book, an up-minus-down spread near zero is the real failure, independent of the −5% print.

The worst asymmetry of all. Diviniti fell 6.3%, in line with the NIFTY, and sits on the group's deepest drawdown (≈15%) — and over its life it has captured more of the market's downside (54%) than its upside (43%). That is the opposite of what the structure is for.

The takeaway for equity long-short: in a flat month every one of these looks fine. September showed which managers are actually running a hedge and which are running the index with a short label on it. Read the up-minus-down column before the headline return.
