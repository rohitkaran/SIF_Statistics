Active asset allocator SIFs in September — the smallest group, the sharpest asymmetry

An allocator long-short fund is meant to dial market exposure up and down with the cycle. In September, one did it brilliantly — qsif's allocator captured 77% of the market's upside but only 6% of its downside — while two others ran the quieter, steadier version of the same mandate. The active asset allocator is the most flexible mandate in the SIF universe: shift net market exposure dynamically — lean in when the odds are good, hedge down when they aren't. Done well, it produces the cleanest shape of all — high participation in rallies, little participation in falls. It is a small group (three schemes with a full month of NAVs), but September gave it the widest spread of outcomes relative to its own promise.

The textbook execution. qsif's allocator is what the category is supposed to look like on a bad day: it kept pace with 77% of the market's up moves over its life yet surrendered only 6% of the down moves — and in September that translated into a +13.75% gain while the index fell 6%. The trade-off is volatility (12.8%): this is an active, high-conviction book, not a low-beta parking spot. But the asymmetry is the best in the universe, and it showed up exactly when it was supposed to.

The steady hands. iSIF and DynaSIF ran the conservative version — downside capture in the low-to-high 20s%, volatility under 5%, drawdowns under 3.5%. Both gave up around a percent in September. They won't produce a +14% month, but they also won't surprise you; their shape is consistent with a fund that keeps net exposure modest and moves it gradually.

For allocators the September read is encouraging: all three protected, and the one that leaned in was positioned correctly. The thing to watch over time is consistency — a 6% down capture is superb, but the test of an allocator is whether it can also be there for the next rally without having de-risked too early. One month, however good, does not settle that.
