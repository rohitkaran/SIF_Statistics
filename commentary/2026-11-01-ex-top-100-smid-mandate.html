Ex-Top 100 long-short SIFs in September — judge them against the midcaps, not the NIFTY

The small- and mid-cap long-shorts have to be measured against the Midcap 50, which fell 7.9% in September. On that yardstick the group delivered a genuine cushion — down a median 2.7% — but the hedge ran from near-total protection to funds that essentially owned the midcaps. The final part of a per-category series.

Last in a series on how each SIF strategy behaved in September's fall, judged against its mandate. ([Hybrids](/commentary/2026-10-29-hybrid-long-short-mandate) · [Equity long-short](/commentary/2026-10-30-equity-long-short-mandate) · [Allocators](/commentary/2026-10-31-allocator-mandate).)

The Ex-Top 100 long-shorts fish outside the large-cap pool — in small- and mid-caps — so comparing them to the NIFTY 50 flatters them. The right benchmark is the NIFTY Midcap 50, which fell 7.91% in September (and is a far wilder ride than the large-caps). The mandate: take long-short positions across the SMID space and cushion that volatility. The capture ratios below are therefore measured against the Midcap 50, not the NIFTY.

On that fair yardstick the group did its job: the median Ex-Top 100 fund fell just 2.7% against the midcaps' −7.9%. But these are structurally more volatile than the hybrids (13%+ volatility is normal here), and the protection was uneven.

The best of the group. DynaSIF Ex-Top 100 actually finished the month up 0.4% while the midcaps fell 8%. Its shape explains why: it has captured essentially all of the midcap upside (105%) but only 58% of the downside — the asymmetry you want from a SMID long-short, if you can tolerate the 15%+ volatility that comes with it. qsif (55% down / 68% up) runs a similar, slightly gentler version.

The ones that mostly own the midcaps. Altiva Ex-Top 100 captured 86% of the midcap downside and 102% of the upside — that is close to a leveraged long midcap position, not a hedge; it fell 4.7%. WSIF (68% down) sits between the two camps. iSIF is roughly symmetric (62/64) — it cushions the level of the fall but offers little selective protection.

The honest read for this category: against the correct benchmark, every one of these funds softened a brutal month for the midcaps, so none "failed" outright. But the gap between DynaSIF's 58% downside capture and Altiva's 86% is the difference between a hedged SMID book and an expensive long one. If you are paying long-short fees to be in small- and mid-caps, that spread is what you are paying for.

Across the whole series, one pattern held in every category: in September the returns were almost a sideshow. What separated the funds was whether their shape — low downside capture, real asymmetry — matched the promise on the label. The best SIFs in each group had it; a handful, in every category, did not.
